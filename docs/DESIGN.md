# Fox & Found — Design

## Core concept

Fox & Found is a finite fox-rescue management and upgrade game built around responsible wildlife rehabilitation.

The player begins with a modest rescue property, limited facilities, basic supplies, and the capacity to handle only relatively straightforward cases. As the rescue demonstrates good welfare decisions and professional competence, it earns stronger funding, improves its facilities, and becomes capable of helping more complicated cases.

The emotional reward comes from helping each individual fox reach the best possible outcome.

## Design philosophy

- Finite progression with a real ending.
- Optional post-win play.
- No prestige or reset treadmill.
- No manipulative real-world waiting mechanics.
- Foxes are treated as individuals.
- Welfare comes before collection, growth, or score.
- Educational information should arise naturally through play.
- Upgrades should unlock real rescue capabilities.
- Cute fox moments are welcome, but foxes should remain recognizably wild animals.
- Organizational failure may happen, but foxes should not be punished for player mistakes.

## Fox roster

The main campaign is planned around approximately **50 hand-authored fox cases**.

Foxes are not procedurally generated. Each fox has a stable rescue ID and an authored case history, personality direction, care needs, and possible story progression.

Accepted foxes arrive under their rescue ID. The player may name them. If the player chooses not to, Harley may assign a name based on personality or distinctive physical traits.

The rescue ID remains the fox's stable internal identifier regardless of display name.

## FOX-001 — Loki

Loki is the tutorial fox and Fox & Found's permanent first resident.

Locked:
- Name: Loki
- Rescue ID: FOX-001
- Species: red fox (*Vulpes vulpes*)
- Permanent sanctuary resident
- Tutorial role
- Already named by Harley before the player begins

Current design direction:
- Human habituation may be the reason Loki cannot be released.
- Loki should be clever, mischievous, and appropriately chaotic.
- Loki introduces care, enrichment, records, enclosure management, and the idea that permanent sanctuary placement can be a successful welfare outcome.

## Harley

Harley is a fictional recurring character named in tribute to the real Harley.

Locked:
- Name: Harley
- Pronouns: they/them
- Nonbinary
- Bisexual
- Wears a small bisexual pride pin as an ordinary part of their outfit
- Their identity is represented naturally rather than treated as a gimmick

Strong current direction, not yet fully locked:
- Harley is the rescue's animal-care / rehabilitation specialist and the player's main recurring coworker.
- They help receive foxes, give progress updates, comment on temperament, suggest enrichment, participate in releases, and provide some of the game's humour.

## Intake system

The player decides when Fox & Found has room and resources for another case, then marks the sanctuary as ready for intake.

A local partner organization contacts Fox & Found with the details currently known about a specific authored fox case.

The player chooses:
- **Accept case**
- **Refer to partner facility**

Referral is not failure. A responsible referral can be the best welfare decision when Fox & Found lacks capacity, equipment, expertise, or appropriate housing.

Because every fox is authored, referred cases can continue through partner-facility updates and reach satisfying outcomes even if they never enter Fox & Found.

## Outcomes

Possible successful outcomes include:
- Release to the wild
- Permanent sanctuary placement
- Transfer to a qualified specialist or partner facility
- Other welfare-appropriate long-term placement

The game should not measure success only by releases.

The central question is: **Did this fox receive appropriate care and the best achievable welfare outcome?**

## Death and difficult outcomes

Tragedy is intentionally limited.

Across roughly 50 authored foxes:
- At most 1–2 foxes may die.
- There are no kit deaths.
- Death does not occur in the player's care.
- The player is never asked to authorize euthanasia.
- Any death occurs at a trusted sister facility and is communicated through partner updates.
- The player is explicitly told they could not have changed the outcome.
- Harley reassures the player that their decision was made with kind intentions and in the animal's best interest.

Possible examples:
- An elderly sanctuary fox dies peacefully after a good life.
- A terminally ill fox is humanely euthanized by the sister facility to prevent suffering.

The euthanasia case should communicate that preventing suffering was itself a compassionate welfare decision.

## Failure philosophy

Failure should affect **Fox & Found as an organization**, not the animals.

If the rescue runs completely out of money or essential supplies:
- Intake closes.
- Current animals are transferred safely to sister facilities before welfare is compromised.
- Their stories continue there.
- Fox & Found experiences a meaningful setback and must rebuild.

The game should warn the player before reaching this state.

## Visual direction

Fox & Found should use real, properly licensed wildlife photography where practical, especially for fox portraits.

Wikimedia Commons is the preferred source.

Do not use generative AI imagery for Harley's game.

The game should clearly state that the photographs depict real animals while the rescue histories and characters are fictional.
