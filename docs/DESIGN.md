# Fox & Found — Design

## Core concept

Fox & Found is a finite fox-rescue management and upgrade game built around responsible wildlife rehabilitation.

The player begins with a modest but legitimate rescue property, limited facilities, basic supplies, and the capacity to handle only relatively straightforward cases. As the rescue demonstrates good welfare decisions and professional competence, it earns stronger funding, improves its facilities, and becomes capable of helping more complicated cases.

## Design principles

- Finite progression with a real ending and optional post-win play.
- No prestige/reset treadmill or manipulative real-world waiting.
- Campaign length is determined by authored content rather than stretched to hit an arbitrary runtime.
- Foxes are treated as individuals and welfare comes before collection, growth, or score.
- Educational information should arise naturally through play.
- Upgrades should unlock real rescue capabilities.
- Routine care should be abstracted rather than turned into repetitive clicking.
- Organizational failure may happen, but foxes should not be punished for player mistakes.
- Use real, properly licensed wildlife photography; do not use generative AI imagery for Harley's game.

## Fox roster

The main campaign is planned around approximately **50 hand-authored fox cases**.

Each fox has a stable rescue ID, authored intake story, rehabilitation needs, personality direction, and possible outcomes. Accepted foxes arrive under their rescue ID. The player may name them; if they do not, Harley may choose a suitable unused name based on personality or distinctive physical traits.

## Case order

Case order is **partially randomized**.

- FOX-001 Loki is always fixed.
- FOX-002 is intended to be the fixed first intake/tutorial case.
- Most later foxes come from curated authored pools.
- Pools may be gated by progression stage and season.
- Some milestone/story cases may appear at fixed progression points.
- The shuffle should avoid repetitive or poorly paced sequences.
- Some cases beyond current capabilities should still appear so referral remains meaningful, but impossible cases should not dominate.

## FOX-001 — Loki

Locked:
- Name: Loki
- Rescue ID: FOX-001
- Species: red fox (*Vulpes vulpes*)
- Permanent sanctuary resident and tutorial fox
- Already named by Harley before the player begins
- Harley has cared for Loki since he was a kit
- Loki is never transferred to a sister facility

Current direction:
- Human habituation may be why Loki cannot be released.
- Loki is clever, mischievous, and appropriately chaotic.
- Loki introduces sanctuary care, enrichment, records, enclosure management, and the idea that permanent sanctuary placement can be a successful welfare outcome.
- If Fox & Found enters emergency closure, Harley personally ensures Loki's continued care.

## Harley

Locked:
- Name: Harley
- Pronouns: they/them
- Nonbinary
- Bisexual
- Wears a small bisexual pride pin as an ordinary part of their outfit
- Their identity is represented naturally rather than treated as a gimmick
- They have cared for Loki since he was a kit

Strong current direction:
- Harley is the rescue's animal-care / rehabilitation specialist and main recurring coworker.

## Starting rescue

At the beginning of Week 1, Fox & Found has:
- Loki's permanent enclosure
- One basic intake/quarantine space
- One basic rehabilitation run
- Basic food storage
- Basic sanitation/supply storage
- Simple camera monitoring
- Small office/management area

Starting practical capacity:
- Loki
- One uncomplicated active rehabilitation case

The rehab run is initially missing one required safety improvement.

### Tutorial upgrade

The player receives startup funding to purchase:
- **Dig-proof perimeter reinforcement**

Once purchased, the rescue can open for its first uncomplicated intake.

## Starting capabilities

Available:
- basic intake
- basic quarantine
- basic rehabilitation
- low-contact care
- camera observation

Not yet available:
- advanced medical care
- contagious-disease isolation
- orphaned-kit care
- multiple simultaneous rehab runs
- specialist long-term sanctuary housing
- advanced transport
- intensive monitoring

These tags are provisional and should be refined as cases and upgrades are authored.

## Intake system

The player decides when Fox & Found has room and resources for another case, then marks the sanctuary as ready for intake.

A partner organization contacts Fox & Found with a specific authored case. The player chooses:
- **Accept case**
- **Refer to partner facility**

Referral is not failure. Because every fox is authored, referred cases can continue through partner-facility updates and still reach satisfying outcomes.

## Outcomes

Possible successful outcomes include:
- Release to the wild
- Permanent sanctuary placement
- Transfer to a qualified specialist or partner facility
- Other welfare-appropriate long-term placement

Success is not measured only by releases.

## Death and difficult outcomes

Across roughly 50 authored foxes:
- At most 1–2 foxes may die.
- No kit deaths.
- Death does not occur in the player's care.
- The player is never asked to authorize euthanasia.
- Any death occurs at a trusted sister facility and is communicated through partner updates.
- The player is explicitly told they could not have changed the outcome.
- Harley reassures the player that their decision was made with kind intentions and in the animal's best interest.
- Humane euthanasia is framed as a compassionate decision that prevented suffering.

## Failure philosophy

If the rescue runs out of money or essential supplies:
- Intake closes.
- Active rehabilitation animals are transferred safely before welfare is compromised.
- Their stories continue there.
- Loki is not transferred; Harley ensures his continued care.
- Fox & Found takes a meaningful but recoverable organizational setback.
